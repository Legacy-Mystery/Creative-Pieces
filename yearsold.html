Creative Non-Fiction

Creative nonfiction is writing about real-life events or memories, just told like a story. These can be about big life moments, childhood memories, family, or how you feel looking back on the past. You can focus on one single event, or jump between different ages and snapshots to show how things have changed. Below is my example looking back at growing up and how memories fade over time.

I am ... years old

     I am 9 years old, at a fast food joint that's not really food. There's a giant metal clown up above the roof of the bathroom. Kids I know are yelling and kids I don't know are trying to get cake before their parents fuss at them. I climb up the dull blue stairs that smell of rubbery plastic before reaching the top, where I can see everyone below from a dome. Once it's time to eat, I slide down the slide, go sit down, and eat my Happy Meal. After eating, it's time for cake. It's a mostly plain cake of vanilla with vanilla frosting and a picture of the clown in the center—really sweet in my memory. It's my brother's 3rd birthday, and it's the beginning of February. We're here because he is too young for anything else. I am happy to be here for him, just as I would want people to be happy to come to mine.

     I am 14. It's now the end of spring, that one period every kid yearns for… Summer break. Except… I am dreading it as it means final report cards are coming home. While I know everyone is still struggling to recover from COVID, it hits me especially hard—helping my mother with my brothers, sister and cousins, causing my grades to plummet as I’m not able to attend my virtual classes. Going back to school in person is hard, the fluorescent lights always humming that dreadful low note, and teachers’ disapproving but slightly concerned looks when they think my back is turned or I’m asleep in class.

     I am 20 now… and I wish I could remember more of the details. It feels like the years have flown past me, leaving behind only hazy memories and half-clear images. I wish I hadn't taken for granted those days of simply being together—the predictable rhythm of family reunions, each side of my family claiming its own dedicated weekend on the calendar year after year.
